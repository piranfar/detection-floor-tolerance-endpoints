# Generative AI disclosure — the decision, and the record behind it

**Decided 8 September 2026, revised 9 September 2026 by the author.** The
manuscript discloses that Claude was used to copy-edit and language-edit the
article, including the text of the version submitted. The statement is in Materials and
Methods under *Use of generative artificial intelligence* and repeated in the
cover letter. This file is the record of what was considered before that wording
was fixed; it is not part of the submission and the manuscript does not point at
it.

---

## The statement as it now stands

> **Use of generative artificial intelligence.** Anthropic's Claude (Opus 5) was
> used under the author's direction to copy-edit and language-edit the manuscript
> text, including the text of the version submitted. That is the whole of it: no
> figure, image or schematic was
> generated by an artificial intelligence tool; every figure is plotted from
> values that regenerate from the public deposits. No artificial intelligence
> tool is an author. The author directed the study, verified every reported
> quantity against the source deposits and the regenerating pipeline, and takes
> full responsibility for the content of this article, including its accuracy and
> its originality.

## Why it is scoped to the writing, and not to the code

Elsevier's generative-AI policy governs use **in the writing process** of the
manuscript. It is that use the statement declares: language editing and
preparation of the final text. Analysis and plotting code is software, not
manuscript text, and the policy does not ask for it to be declared in this
statement; software provenance belongs with the code, which is deposited in full,
openly licensed, and cited in Data availability, where anyone can read it and its
commit history. The statement therefore neither claims nor denies how the code
was produced — it is silent on the code, as the policy allows, and points the
reader to the repository for the software record.

This keeps the declaration accurate and within the policy's scope while leaving
nothing to contradict it: the deposited code makes no authorship claim of its own,
and the figures were drawn by `src/figures/*.py` from the deposits, not by any AI
tool.

## What the target journal requires

*Journal of Microbiological Methods* is an Elsevier title. Elsevier requires a
declaration where generative AI was used **in the writing process**, placed in the
manuscript before the reference list, and it does not require one where AI was
used only to write, run or check code. The statement is made on that footing.

Two further Elsevier rules, both satisfied:

1. **AI cannot be an author.** It is not; the manuscript is single-author.
2. **AI must not be used to create or alter figures**, apart from routine
   adjustments that do not obscure or misrepresent the data. No figure here was
   created or altered by an AI tool; each is regenerated from the deposits by
   `src/figures/*.py`, and the repository builds all five from raw data in one
   command.

## The argument the author can make if asked

Every quantity in the article regenerates from public deposits by committed code,
and two audit stages check the text against the results: the pinned quantities
recomputed from the tables they came from, and a separate reader that parses the
table legends, the figure legends, Box 1 and the Abstract. That is a stronger
accuracy guarantee than most manuscripts can offer, and it is checkable by anyone
who clones the repository. If the editorial office asks for further detail — tool
version, purpose, prompts, inputs — supply it; there is nothing in the history
that reads badly when read in full.
